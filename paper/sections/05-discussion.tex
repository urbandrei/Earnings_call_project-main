\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Four statements hold on the completed ladder. Strong econometric baselines are hard
to beat, and \Rtwo{} vs.\ persistence exposes a regime failure that raw MSE hides.
Frozen text content adds nothing over past volatility, for a measurable reason:
call embeddings identify the company, and where companies recur a same-company
transcript swap leaves the head unchanged. Frozen audio content is inert beyond
past volatility on 40\,kbps transcodes and 64\,kbps webcasts alike, while still
encoding the speaker's company. Fusion does not rescue the modalities it fuses.
Each statement depends on apparatus the shared substrate lacks. Applied to the
field's own code and data, that apparatus returns the same
diagnosis: what reproduces is price-only or same-company history, and the text
models lose their advantage when the company is unseen. The strongest recent
positive claims \citep{soundofrisk2025,cao2024ecc} report no identity controls and
release no code; \texttt{audit predictions} makes the check cheap for any author
with a prediction file. The verdict is not that transcripts and
audio carry no information about a company's risk, but that the benchmarks this
literature uses cannot tell that information apart from knowing which company is
speaking, and that once they can, the past-volatility floor has not been beaten.
