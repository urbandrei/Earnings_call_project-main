\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Four things hold across the whole set of models. Classical volatility forecasts
are hard to beat, and measuring against persistence exposes a regime failure that
raw MSE hides. Text features add nothing to past volatility, because the
embeddings identify the company, and where companies recur in training, swapping
in another call by the same company changes nothing. Audio features add nothing
either, at 40\,kbps or 64\,kbps, even though they too identify the company, and
combining them does not help. None of this could be measured with the files the
field has been using, which is the gap the benchmark fills. The same checks on
five published models give the same answer. What reproduces is price-only models
or same-company history, and the text models lose their edge as soon as the
company is new. The strongest recent positive results
\citep{soundofrisk2025,cao2024ecc} could not be run through these checks because
their code is not released; the \texttt{audit predictions} command makes that
check cheap for anyone with a file of predictions. We do not claim that
transcripts and audio carry no information about a company's risk. We claim that
the benchmarks in use cannot tell that information apart from knowing which
company is speaking, and that once they can, nothing yet built beats a forecast
from past volatility. That is the baseline the next model has to clear.
