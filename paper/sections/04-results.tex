\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The tables in this section use FinCall-Surprise, the $\Delta v$ target and the
trading-day definition. MAEC, the level target, MSE, the calendar-day definition
and every other model are in Appendix~\ref{app:complete} and in the supplement,
regenerated from the released CSVs.

\paragraph{The baseline to beat.}
HAR-RV is strong everywhere except one cell (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). On the FinCall
temporal split at $\tau{=}30$, the training period includes the COVID spike and
the test year (2021) is calm, so every long-horizon forecast of the volatility
level, ours and every content model, does worse than persistence. The same models
do fine on the \tickerdisjoint{} split and on MAEC, so we treat this as a regime
effect and note it. On the measured anchor a constant (the training mean) already
scores 0.17, 0.16, 0.06 and 0.09 on the temporal split and 0.20, 0.20, 0.13 and
0.04 on the \tickerdisjoint{} split. That is the reference for every $\Delta v$
claim below.

\paragraph{Text models read the company, not the call (Table~\ref{tab:controls}).}
The text-only ridge stays within 0.14 of the constant on both splits and is below
HAR-RV by at least 0.09 in seven of eight cells (the eighth is the COVID cell).
The MLPs overfit. Adding text features to the past-volatility ridge makes it
\emph{worse} in all eight cells. A linear probe recovers the ticker from the frozen
call embedding 89.5\% of the time on FinCall and 53.9\% on MAEC. On the temporal
split, where test companies also appear in training, giving each test call the
transcript of \emph{another call by the same company} changes the score by less
than 0.03, while a random transcript hurts. The
model is reading which company is speaking. On the \tickerdisjoint{} split the
same-company swap does hurt at $\tau\ge15$, so there is some call-specific signal
for companies the model has never seen, but it stays below the past-volatility
baseline and disappears once past volatility is in the model (0.301 against 0.318
at $\tau{=}30$). MAEC shows the same pattern.

\paragraph{Audio adds nothing to past volatility.}
Audio-only ridges on eGeMAPS, WavLM-Large and emotion2vec+ stay within 0.10 of the
constant at every horizon on both splits and never beat it by more than 0.02.
Adding WavLM to the past-volatility ridge makes it worse in all eight cells. In the
audio-swap check (Table~\ref{tab:controls}, bottom), the model with past volatility
keeps its score at $\tau{=}3$ when every test call is given a \emph{random other
company's} audio (0.456 to 0.453). At longer horizons the swap costs more, but the
model with real audio is still below the ridge with past volatility alone, so
whatever the audio contributes does not add to the baseline. A probe still
recovers the ticker from pooled WavLM embeddings 76.3\% of the time. A gender
check (dominant-speaker pitch proxy) finds no error gap (test MSE 0.243 against
0.239). Rerun on Earnings25 by bitrate, the gain from real over randomly swapped
audio never exceeds 0.03, is negative in 7 of 12 cells, and is no larger at
64\,kbps than at lower bitrates (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:earnings25-audio}). The
FinCall result is not an artefact of its 40\,kbps audio.

\paragraph{Combining modalities.}
Gated fusion is below the ridge heads with past volatility in 15 of 16 cells. The
stacking model is the best content model but stays below HAR-RV in six of eight
cells. It beats HAR-RV only in the COVID cell (0.100, still below the
past-volatility ridge at 0.113) and at \tickerdisjoint{} $\tau{=}30$ (0.396 against
0.345, Holm $p{=}0.003$ against Stage~1, with no pre-registered test against
HAR-RV). It beats the text and audio stages in seven of eight cells and the
past-volatility ridge in two. So a multimodal
model is not better than the best single-input model when that input is past
volatility, and the condition we set in advance for a positive paper (gains
surviving the checks on at least two of four horizons) was not met.

\paragraph{The field's own models (Table~\ref{tab:repro-main}).}
Among the five papers we could run, the results that reproduce belong to
price-only or same-company models (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
Same-Company-Same-Signal reproduces from its shipped data. TSMixer matches to
$10^{-5}$ and TMLP within 0.03 (including their own within-company swap result),
and its training-free same-company history comes within 0.05 of KeFVP's published
MSE at every horizon on EC and MAEC-15 with no text and no training, a model that
has no meaning once companies are held out. KeFVP, a price-series model
conditioned on text with its audio branch disabled in its own code, reproduces
seven of eight MAEC cells within one published standard deviation. Its EC row, run
without the knowledge-enhanced embeddings that are no longer available, is above
all four published cells and we do not count it. HTML's released driver shuffles
features and labels independently without a seed, so it cannot have produced the
published table, and with the authors' model classes unchanged the published
recipe reaches the published 15- and 30-day values on no split. The published
ensemble of \citet{sawhney2020mm} is matched at 7 days and beyond only because its
untrained text and audio branches act as an intercept on a finance SVR whose
mixing weights the code tunes on the test set. Under our checks
(Table~\ref{tab:repro-main}), holding companies out costs the three text models
12--82\% in MSE while TSMixer and KeFVP lose nothing, although their training sets
are as large (EC) or cut by the same third
(DEC, MAEC). The gap between training and test moves models by at most 13\%.
HTML's own trained model changes by at most 4\% when a test transcript is swapped
for another call by the same company, against 9--23\% for a different company's
call. What HTML reads from a transcript is which company is speaking.

\paragraph{After the cutoff (Table~\ref{tab:lookahead}).}
We scored the FinCall-trained models, unchanged, on 475 Earnings25 calls from late
2025, after every model's training cutoff and on companies none had seen. On the
level target, no model that was competitive before the cutoff got worse. HAR-RV
rose from 0.44, 0.37, 0.14 and $-$0.29 to 0.60, 0.54, 0.35 and 0.08, and the
content ridges rose with it (only the fusion ridge is unstable, at $\tau{=}30$).
Levels are not comparable across the cutoff (different years and companies), but
the ordering is, and it holds. HAR-RV beats every content model at every horizon,
and no content model that was competitive before the cutoff collapses after it.
Under the call-date anchor of the published label sets, the same frozen models
collapse before the cutoff (text plus past volatility on $\Delta v$ falls to
between $-1.7$ and $-3.6$ at 3 to 15 days, Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:lookahead-assumed}), because
the reaction session then sits inside the past-volatility features.
