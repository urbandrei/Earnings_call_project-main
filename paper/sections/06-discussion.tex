\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Across every model we trained, the same picture holds. Text features do not improve
on past volatility alone, and the swap checks say why. A probe reads the ticker
out of the call embedding nine times in ten, and where a company recurs in
training, handing the model a different call by that company moves its score by
less than 0.03. Audio identifies the company without improving the forecast, and
the one stacking gain over the price-only reference appears only on a split that
is not in date order. The shared test files could not have shown any of this, because
they carry no company identity for the audio corpus, no company-disjoint split
and no measured day~0. The controlled reruns show large company
sensitivity for HTML, the Sawhney ensemble and the SCSS text model, no
consistent change for the price-dominated models, and an inconclusive port for
DialogueGAT. Two recent positive results \citep{soundofrisk2025,cao2024ecc}
release no code and could not be checked. Transcripts and audio may well
carry information about a company's risk. The test files in use cannot separate that
information from knowing which company is speaking. Once they can, under the
temporal and date-ordered company-disjoint protocols none of the content models
evaluated here improves on its past-volatility reference by the 2\% margin
fixed in advance, and the isolated gains that remain need targeted
validation. That is the bar the next model has to clear.
