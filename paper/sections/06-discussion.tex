\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Across every model we trained, the same picture holds. HAR-RV and the other
classical forecasts are hard to beat, and scoring against persistence exposes a
regime failure at the 30-day horizon that raw MSE would have hidden. Text
features do not improve on past volatility alone, and the swap checks say why. A probe reads the ticker out of the call embedding nine times in ten,
and where a company recurs in training, handing the model a different call by
that company moves its score by less than 0.03. Audio behaves the same way at
40\,kbps and at 64\,kbps, identifying the company without improving the forecast,
and the one stacking gain over the matched model appears only on a split that is
not in date order. The shared test files could not have shown any of this, because they
carry no company identity for the audio corpus, no company-disjoint split and no
measured day~0. Applied to five published models on their own data, the checks
give the same answer. The price-based models keep their accuracy when
companies are held out, and the error of the text models rises by 12--82\%. The
two strongest recent positive results \citep{soundofrisk2025,cao2024ecc} could
not be checked, since their code is not released, though the
\texttt{audit predictions} command would make the company-level part of that
check a matter of minutes for anyone holding a file of predictions. Transcripts and audio may well carry
information about a company's risk. The test files in use cannot separate that
information from knowing which company is speaking. Once they can, none of the
content models evaluated here improves on a matched past-volatility model by the
margin we fixed in advance. That is the bar the next model has to clear.
