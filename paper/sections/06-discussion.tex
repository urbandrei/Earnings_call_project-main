\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Across every model we trained, the same picture holds. HAR-RV and the other
classical forecasts are hard to beat, and scoring against persistence exposes a
regime failure at the 30-day horizon that raw MSE would have hidden. Text
features add nothing once past volatility is in the model, and the swap checks
say why. A probe reads the ticker out of the call embedding nine times in ten,
and where a company recurs in training, handing the model a different call by
that company moves its score by less than 0.03. Audio behaves the same way at
40\,kbps and at 64\,kbps, identifying the company without improving the forecast,
and stacking the two modalities beats HAR-RV in only one cell outside the COVID
regime. The shared test files could not have shown any of this, because they
carry no company identity for the audio corpus, no company-disjoint split and no
measured day~0. Applied to five published models on their own data, the checks
give the same answer. The price-based models keep their accuracy when
companies are held out, and the text models lose 12--82\% of theirs. The
two strongest recent positive results \citep{soundofrisk2025,cao2024ecc} could
not be checked at all, since their code is not released, though the
\texttt{audit predictions} command would make that check a matter of minutes for
anyone holding a file of predictions. Transcripts and audio may well carry information about a company's risk. The test files in use can't separate that information from the company, and once they can, the existing models can't beat a forecast from past volatility. That is the bar the next model has to clear.
