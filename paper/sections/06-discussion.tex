\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Across every model we trained, the same picture holds. The classical forecasts
are hard to beat, and scoring against persistence exposes a regime failure at
the 30-day horizon that raw MSE would have hidden. Text features do not improve
on past volatility alone, and the swap checks say why. A probe reads the ticker
out of the call embedding nine times in ten, and where a company recurs in
training, handing the model a different call by that company moves its score by
less than 0.03. Audio identifies the company without improving the forecast, and
the one stacking gain over the matched model appears only on a split that is not
in date order. The shared test files could not have shown any of this, because
they carry no company identity for the audio corpus, no company-disjoint split
and no measured day~0. Five published models give the same answer under the
checks. Two recent positive results \citep{soundofrisk2025,cao2024ecc} could not
be checked, since their code is not released, though the audit command covers
the company-level part from a prediction file. Transcripts and audio may well
carry information about a company's risk. The test files in use cannot separate that
information from knowing which company is speaking. Once they can, none of the
content models evaluated here improves on a matched past-volatility model by the
margin we fixed in advance, and the isolated gains that remain need targeted
validation. That is the bar the next model has to clear.
