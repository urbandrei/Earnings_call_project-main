\section*{Limitations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Limitations}

\textbf{One post-cutoff period.} The post-cutoff test rests on 475 calls from
five calm months of 2025, 470 of them from October to December. Period, companies and source all change at once, so it is a
temporal external validation of the ordering of models, not a measurement of
pretraining contamination, and 59\% of its companies appear in FinCall's training
split. \textbf{Small date-ordered sets.} The split that is both date-ordered and
company-disjoint keeps 97 FinCall test calls and only 22 FinCall and 5 MAEC
validation calls, on which ridge penalties are chosen and the stack is fit, so
model selection and stacking are least stable there. The intervals resample
companies but not calendar time, so shocks common to one quarter are not
accounted for. \textbf{Forward-looking target.} It assumes a
call after a pre-open release ends before that day's close, checked on
Earnings25 only, and it was run on FinCall for the canonical models only.
\textbf{Reproductions run on their own data}, not on \ecvol-bench, to keep ``the
model fails'' separable from ``the model does not transfer''. Two carry known
costs. KeFVP's released EC embeddings are unavailable upstream, and DialogueGAT's
corpus was never released, so our port stops at its protocol. \textbf{MLP heads
are seed-unstable} (standard deviation of \Rtwo{} across seeds up to 6.8 on the
level target and 1.1 on $\Delta v$), which is why the grid uses deterministic
models. MLP cells in the appendix are seed means with that caveat.
\textbf{Short-horizon \Rtwo{} inflation.} The persistence denominator is noisiest
at $\tau{=}3$ (its MSE is about 2.8 times that at $\tau{=}7$), so short-horizon
gains are easier to show. \textbf{Timestamps.} 10.7\% of FinCall and 31.4\% of
MAEC calls (13.0\% of the MAEC calls that carry targets) have no Item~2.02 filing
in the window and use a recorded fallback tier, and intraday releases are treated
as after-hours. Results under the
call-date anchor of prior label sets are not interchangeable with ours, and the
call-date targets are shipped as a variant. \textbf{Regime sensitivity.}
FinCall-Surprise spans COVID. The temporal $\tau{=}30$ failure shows that
long-horizon level forecasts are regime-sensitive, which is why we emphasize
$\Delta v$ and per-year breakdowns. \textbf{MAEC coverage.} Its 78.1\% price-join
rate (2015--2018 delistings) makes it a secondary corpus, and it ships no raw
audio. \textbf{Source audio.} Every FinCall file is a 40\,kbps MP3 transcode and
Earnings25 is 64\,kbps or lower, so no result here is established on lossless
audio. \textbf{No diarization.} Audio is pooled per call, and the gender analysis
uses a dominant-speaker pitch proxy, so a small gap on that proxy does not
establish the absence of one. \textbf{Identity reconstruction is
imperfect.} 7.1\% of FinCall calls remain unresolved and are excluded with a
reason code, and nine resolved training calls carry a date their transcript
contradicts.
\textbf{No fine-tuned or LLM-based content model of our own.} Stages~2 to~4 use frozen encoders with ridge and MLP heads. The HTML reproduction, a trained hierarchical transformer over frozen BERT features, shows the same dependence on company identity
(Table~\ref{tab:repro-main}), so the pattern is not an artifact of a linear head. Whether a fine-tuned encoder
behaves differently is untested here. \S\ref{sec:recommendations} points to structured features extracted by language models as the place to look. The audit command lets anyone check such a model from its prediction file.
