\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report results factually and defer interpretation to \S\ref{sec:discussion}.
Curated headline tables appear here on the identity-robust \dv{} target for
FinCall-Surprise under the trading-day convention; the complete grid (both corpora,
level-$v$ and \dv, \Rtwo{} and MSE, all splits and horizons) is in
Appendix~\ref{app:complete}. Stars denote Diebold--Mariano significance
($p<0.05$): in Table~\ref{tab:baselines} vs.\ persistence, and in
Tables~\ref{tab:text} and~\ref{tab:audio} vs.\ the Stage-1 ticker-FE GBDT; in the
consolidated grid (Table~\ref{tab:grid}) markers are Holm-corrected across horizons
and signed ($*$ better / \dag{} worse than Stage-1).

\subsection{Baselines (Stages 0--1)}
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} shows the econometric floor. HAR-RV is strong at short and
medium horizons on both splits, confirming RQ1's premise that past-volatility
baselines are hard to beat. The one striking failure is the FinCall \emph{temporal}
split at $\tau{=}30$, where every model falls below persistence (HAR-RV
$\Rtwo=-0.287$): the training window is COVID-inflated (Feb--May~2020) while the
test window is calm late~2021, so a long-horizon level forecast trained on the
crisis regime is mis-calibrated. The same models pass comfortably on the
\tickerdisjoint{} split (HAR-RV $\Rtwo=+0.229$ at $\tau{=}30$) and on MAEC, so we
treat this as a documented regime exception rather than a target bug; per-year
breakdowns are mandatory in the appendix.

\subsection{Text content (Stage 2)}
Table~\ref{tab:text} shows frozen-text heads against the Stage-1 baseline on \dv.
The result is a clean negative: \emph{text content does not cleanly beat the
identity/past-volatility baselines}. On the FinCall temporal split the text ridge
head is near zero and collapses at $\tau{=}30$; the MLP heads overfit badly. On the
\tickerdisjoint{} split the text ridge head is weak ($\Rtwo$ between $-0.04$ and
$+0.17$) while the past-vol-only head is consistently strong
($\Rtwo\approx0.19$--$0.45$), so any apparent text gain is not separable from what
past volatility already provides. MAEC shows the same pattern at smaller magnitude
(Appendix~\ref{app:complete}).

\subsection{Identity controls (Stage 2 diagnostic)}
Table~\ref{tab:controls} explains the negative result. A linear probe recovers the
ticker from frozen call embeddings at 89.5\% on FinCall (319$\times$ chance) and
53.9\% on MAEC (487$\times$ chance): identity is heavily encoded. The transcript
shuffle is decisive---replacing each test call's transcript with a \emph{different
call from the same ticker} barely changes \Rtwo, whereas a global shuffle degrades
it. The heads are reading \emph{which company} is speaking, not \emph{what} is said.
This is the in-corpus confirmation of \citet{scss2024} and the reason RQ4 is
central---and it is only measurable because the benchmark reconstructed the
identity the corpus omits (\S\ref{sec:corpora}).

\subsection{Audio content (Stage 3)}
Table~\ref{tab:audio} extends the same design to audio. Audio-\emph{only} ridge heads
(eGeMAPS, WavLM-Large, emotion2vec+) sit at \Rtwo{} zero on the temporal and
\tickerdisjoint{} splits ($-0.06$ to $+0.03$), and their MLP counterparts fall
well below persistence at long horizons (to $-1.3$ at $\tau{=}30$): frozen audio
content alone carries no measurable \dv{} signal. Adding past-volatility covariates reproduces the Stage-2
pattern---the only DM-significant wins over Stage-1 are past-vol-augmented heads on
the \tickerdisjoint{} split. The audio-shuffle control (bottom block) is decisive:
replacing each test call's audio with a random \emph{other company's} call leaves
most of those gains intact ($\tau{=}3$: $0.337\!\to\!0.318$; $\tau{=}30$:
$0.212\!\to\!0.100$), so the audio features are inert and past volatility carries
the signal. Identity is nonetheless encoded: a linear probe recovers the ticker from
per-call WavLM embeddings at 76.3\% (271$\times$ chance; emotion2vec+ 29.4\%,
105$\times$). An exploratory gender-confound check (eGeMAPS-F0 dominant-speaker
proxy, 100\% coverage) finds near-identical per-group test MSE (0.241 low-pitch
vs.\ 0.242 high-pitch) and a 0.03 correlation between speaker F0 and squared error:
with inert audio features, no large gender disparity is detectable (see the Ethics
Statement for caveats). All of this rests on FinCall-Surprise's 40\,kbps source
audio; \S\ref{sec:results-clean-audio} is the test of whether it transfers.

\subsection{Fusion and the consolidated grid (Stage 4)}
Gated fusion and late-fusion stacking over all frozen modalities answer RQ2 in the
negative: \emph{multimodal is not better than the best unimodal model}. Only one of
48 \dv{} test cells beats Stages 1--3 DM-significantly (gated fusion with
past-vol covariates, \tickerdisjoint{} $\tau{=}7$, $\Rtwo{=}+0.019$---negligible), and the best fusion cell overall
(stacking, \tickerdisjoint{} $\tau{=}3$, $\Rtwo{=}0.436$) is not significant
vs.\ Stage-1 ($p{=}0.13$). Table~\ref{tab:grid} consolidates the ladder with one
pre-registered canonical model per stage and Holm-corrected confirmatory tests. Two
facts summarise it. On the regime-honest \emph{temporal} split, no content stage
beats the econometric floor at any horizon; where a content model is
Holm-significantly better than Stage-1 (fusion at $\tau{=}30$) it merely beats a
collapsed GBDT while remaining below persistence. On the \emph{\tickerdisjoint}
split the favorable cells are exactly the past-vol-augmented ones that the shuffle
controls (Tables~\ref{tab:controls} and~\ref{tab:audio}) show to be
identity/past-vol-confounded. The per-year breakdown sharpens the regime point: the
FinCall temporal test year is entirely 2021, and there every content model
underperforms persistence on level-$v$ (HAR $-0.29$, GBDT $-0.82$, text $-1.09$,
audio $-1.66$, fusion $-1.02$).

\subsection{Horizon convention: calendar vs.\ trading days}
\label{sec:results-convention}
Table~\ref{tab:convention} reports the delta between the trading-day targets used
above and the calendar-day targets of \citet{qin2019what}. Three facts matter for
anyone comparing to the published leaderboard. First, the calendar window holds
about $0.7\tau$ sessions, and at $\tau{=}3$ it holds at most one session for any
Thursday or Friday call: 48.8\% of FinCall's calls have no defensible $\tau{=}3$
calendar-day target and are excluded under our rules, whereas the released label
sets carry a value for every call. Second, the two conventions are far from
interchangeable at short horizons (correlation of $v_{\mathrm{post}}$ 0.75 at
$\tau{=}3$, 0.93--0.96 beyond), and the persistence MSE---the leaderboard's
metric---roughly doubles at $\tau{=}3$ under calendar days because the target is
averaged over two returns instead of three. A published MSE therefore carries no
information about model quality unless the convention is stated. Third, the
one cell where our baselines fail the pre-registered sanity gate (HAR-RV below
persistence at $\tau{=}30$ on FinCall's temporal split, the COVID-regime cell)
becomes a marginal pass under the calendar convention ($R^2_{\mathrm{OOS}}$
$-0.287 \to +0.017$), because a 21-session window straddles less of the regime
shift than a 30-session one. The Stage-0/1 grid is shipped under both
conventions (\texttt{result\_table\_1}, convention column); the Stage-2--4
content models are evaluated under the trading-day convention only, since the
delta above is a property of the target, not of the head.

\subsection{Prior models under the new controls (Stage R)}
\label{sec:results-repro}
Table~\ref{tab:repro} reports the first entry of the reproduction study: the HTML
sentence-level transformer with its multi-task loss and published hyper-parameters
\citep{yang2020html}, run over our frozen BGE-M3 chunk sequences (a documented
encoder substitution; the head and protocol are the paper's) on EC. Three things
follow. First, the published number is only partly reproduced: at $\tau{=}3$ the
head beats it (0.77 vs.\ 1.18), but the published $\tau{=}15$ and $\tau{=}30$
values (0.153, 0.133) are \emph{below the persistence floor of the shipped labels}
(0.340, 0.202) and no configuration we ran approaches them---the head is worse than
persistence at $\tau{=}30$ in every condition, as every model in
\S\ref{sec:results} is on FinCall. Second, label source barely matters
(0.77/0.44/0.29/0.22 vs.\ 0.74/0.40/0.28/0.22), so the convention and anchor
issues of \S\ref{sec:substrate} do not explain the shortfall. Third, the
\tickerdisjoint{} split raises the head's MSE by 9\% / 23\% / 46\% / 36\% over the
published split with identical labels, collapsing its $R^2_{\mathrm{OOS}}$ against
persistence at $\tau{=}15$ from 0.21 to 0.04, whereas the 30-session-embargoed
temporal split costs nothing---the company-overlap confound, not the embargo, is
what the published protocol leaves unmeasured. The second entry is Same-Company-Same-Signal \citep{scss2024} on its own DEC data: its training-free PEV and STPEV baselines reproduce to three decimals from the shipped file (2023 quarterly averages 0.240 / 0.253 / 0.227 / 0.246 for STPEV against the published 0.239 / 0.253 / 0.227 / 0.246), which confirms the paper's own point in the strongest form: STPEV is same-company history and has \emph{no definition} under a \tickerdisjoint{} evaluation, so the best model on DEC is one that cannot be run in the condition our controls require. Of seventeen papers in the lineage, one ships code that runs as released, seven ship code that needs repair or missing inputs before it starts, and nine ship nothing usable (Table~\ref{tab:code-audit}, Appendix~\ref{app:complete}). The next subsection goes further for the five with usable code.

\subsection{Reproductions with the authors' own code}
\label{sec:results-faithful}
Table~\ref{tab:faithful} re-runs the five papers with usable code on their own data,
with the authors' code wherever it runs and a line-by-line port where it cannot
(\S\ref{sec:repro-protocol}); the fidelity ledger released with the code records every
patch and substitution. The outcomes span the whole range. \textbf{Same-Company-Same-Signal}
reproduces: its TSMixer baseline matches the 76 published cells to a median $10^{-5}$
from the shipped data alone, and its TMLP text model on the authors' OpenAI embeddings
matches per-window means within 0.01 and recovers the paper's own finding that
embeddings shuffled \emph{within} a company do as well as the real ones. \textbf{HTML}
does not: the released driver splits features and labels with three independent
unseeded shuffles, so it cannot have produced the published table, and with the
authors' model classes verbatim on rebuilt inputs the published recipe (ten epochs,
learning rate $2{\times}10^{-5}$, raw log-volatility targets) is still converging at
epoch ten---it beats the paper only at $\tau{=}3$ on a random split with dropout 0,
never reaches persistence at $\tau\ge 15$ on any split, and on the VolTAGE split that
every later paper inherited it is worse than persistence at every horizon. The
text$+$audio row is the one HTML cell that does reproduce: with the 27 Praat
features concatenated, the shipped-code condition gives 0.658/0.347/0.244/0.165
against the published 0.845/0.349/0.251/0.158---and the same inputs under the
paper's chronological split or the VolTAGE split do not, so what reproduces is the
random-split protocol the code implements, not the chronological one the paper
describes.
\textbf{Sawhney et al.} cannot run as released (an unshipped call inventory, price files
and lexicons, a TensorFlow~2.1 stack), so we port it: the finance SVR alone gives
0.712/0.370/0.218/0.154, the text and audio branches are untrained after the two and
one epochs the scripts prescribe, and the published ensemble numbers are reproduced
\emph{because} the released code tunes the mixing weights on the test set, where an
untrained branch's near-constant output acts as an intercept. \textbf{DialogueGAT}'s
corpus and labels were never released; ported onto FinCall's speaker turns under the
paper's per-year protocol it beats persistence at short horizons and collapses on the
2020 split like every model in \S\ref{sec:results}. \textbf{KeFVP}'s released script
needed six import-level repairs before it would start (a missing function, a package
initialiser importing undefined names, a dropped class header, an unreleased package);
its MAEC rows use sentence embeddings regenerated with the authors' own generator
because the released ones were never published \pending{K3 running; EC row waits for a
1.2\,GB file that only a browser session can download}. The pattern the substrate audit
predicted holds on the field's own code: the results that reproduce are those of
price-only or same-company baselines, and the neural claims either do not reproduce or
reproduce for reasons that have nothing to do with the modality they credit.

\subsection{Audio by bitrate: the Stage-3 ladder on Earnings25}
\label{sec:results-clean-audio}
Earnings25 audio is webcast MP3 at 64 kbps for 281 calls and at 24 kbps or
below for 232 (\S\ref{sec:corpora}), so it cannot test ``clean'' audio; it can
test whether the FinCall audio result depends on bitrate \emph{within one quarter},
with the same extractors, heads and controls as Table~\ref{tab:audio}. A
single-quarter corpus spans about 105 sessions, so the embargoed temporal split
degenerates (five training calls); Table~\ref{tab:earnings25-audio} therefore uses
the ticker-disjoint split, pooled and per stratum. Audio-plus-past-vol heads beat
persistence on $\Delta v$ (pooled $R^2_{\mathrm{OOS}}$ 0.44 at $\tau{=}3$), but a
model given a \emph{random other call's} audio keeps most of that (0.35): the
audio increment is 0.09 at $\tau{=}3$ and at most 0.05 beyond, and per stratum it
is small and sign-inconsistent (64 kbps: 0.03 / 0.00 / 0.08 / $-$0.02; $\le$24 kbps:
$-$0.04 / 0.02 / 0.07 / 0.04). With 39 and 55 test calls per stratum these
increments are not distinguishable from zero, and the higher-bitrate stratum is
not the better one. The FinCall result therefore does not look like an artefact
of 40 kbps transcodes: at 64 kbps, on 2025 calls, with a post-cutoff encoder
stack, audio is as inert beyond past volatility as it was on FinCall.

\subsection{Post-cutoff lookahead}
\label{sec:results-lookahead}
Every head in Tables~\ref{tab:baselines}--\ref{tab:grid} was fit on FinCall's
temporal training split; here the same fits are scored, with no retraining and no
threshold change, on the 475 Earnings25 calls (held July--December 2025, 97\% of
them in October--December)---after the training cut-off of every frozen encoder in
the stack, and on companies none of the heads has seen. Table~\ref{tab:lookahead}
shows no post-cutoff degradation for any head that was competitive in-cutoff: HAR-RV
rises from 0.46/0.41/0.21/$-$0.29 to 0.56/0.47/0.32/0.08, and the ridge text and
audio heads rise with it. The two segments differ in period and universe (the
FinCall test year is 2021, mid-cap-heavy; Earnings25 is S\&P~500 in a calm
quarter), so absolute levels are not comparable across the cut-off; what is
comparable is the \emph{ordering}, and it survives: HAR-RV beats every content head
at every horizon on level-$v$, the content heads track past volatility rather than
adding to it (on $\Delta v$ the frozen text head reaches 0.25 at $\tau{=}3$ against
HAR's 0.56), and at $\tau{=}30$ only HAR-RV edges past persistence (0.08, CI
$-0.12$ to $0.47$). The one head that does degrade is the 3k-dimensional fusion
ridge (level-$v$ $\tau{=}3$: 0.43 to 0.15, CI $-0.15$ to $0.67$), which is unstable
rather than contaminated. A lookahead effect would show as content heads that were
competitive in-cutoff and collapse post-cutoff; we observe none. This is, to our
knowledge, the first frozen-pipeline post-cutoff evaluation in this literature; the
ticker fixed-effect GBDT of Stage~1 is absent by construction, since every
post-cutoff company is unseen.

\paragraph{Anchor sensitivity of the frozen heads.}
Table~\ref{tab:lookahead-measured} repeats the evaluation with day~0 anchored on
the measured EDGAR timestamp on both sides of the cut-off
(\texttt{result\_table\_7\_measured.csv}; same heads, same splits). HAR-RV barely
moves, but the ridge heads that carry a learned past-volatility block change
character: their in-cutoff $\Delta v$ collapse under the after-hours anchor
($-1.7$ to $-3.6$ for text, $-1.0$ to $-2.0$ for WavLM) disappears under the
measured one ($+0.04$ to $+0.34$), and the post-cutoff fusion instability shrinks
from $-7.0$ to $-0.8$ at $\tau{=}3$. This is consistent with the learned
$[v_{\mathrm{pre}}, \mathrm{RV}]$ block absorbing the reaction session that the
after-hours rule leaves inside the pre-window for before-open calls, and it
identifies the ``past-vol ridge is not HAR'' gap of the Limitations as at least
partly an anchoring artefact. The ordering is unchanged on level-$v$; on
$\Delta v$ at $\tau{=}30$ the frozen WavLM+past-vol head is the only one whose
post-cutoff CI excludes zero (0.18, CI 0.14--0.24, against HAR's 0.08), a
single-quarter, no-DM-test observation we report as exploratory and not as a win.
The variant is an input to the re-baselining decision of \S\ref{sec:timing}, not a
change of the primary targets.
