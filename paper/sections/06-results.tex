\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report results factually and defer the headline framing to
\S\ref{sec:discussion}. Curated headline tables appear here on the identity-robust
\dv{} target for FinCall-Surprise; the complete grid (both corpora, level-$v$ and
\dv, \Rtwo{} and MSE, all splits and horizons) is in
Appendix~\ref{app:complete}. Stars denote Diebold--Mariano significance
($p<0.05$): in Table~\ref{tab:baselines} vs.\ persistence, and in
Tables~\ref{tab:text} and~\ref{tab:audio} vs.\ the Stage-1 ticker-FE GBDT; in the
consolidated grid (Table~\ref{tab:grid}) markers are Holm-corrected across horizons
and signed ($*$ better / \dag{} worse than Stage-1).

\subsection{Baselines (Stages 0--1)}
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} shows the econometric floor. HAR-RV is strong at short and
medium horizons on both splits, confirming RQ1's premise that past-volatility
baselines are hard to beat. The one striking failure is the FinCall \emph{temporal}
split at $\tau{=}30$, where every model falls below persistence (HAR-RV
$\Rtwo=-0.287$): the training window is COVID-inflated (Feb--May~2020) while the
test window is calm late~2021, so a long-horizon level forecast trained on the
crisis regime is mis-calibrated. The same models pass comfortably on the
\tickerdisjoint{} split (HAR-RV $\Rtwo=+0.229$ at $\tau{=}30$) and on MAEC, so we
treat this as a documented regime exception rather than a target bug; per-year
breakdowns are mandatory in the appendix.

\subsection{Text content (Stage 2)}
Table~\ref{tab:text} shows frozen-text heads against the Stage-1 baseline on \dv.
The result is a clean negative: \emph{text content does not cleanly beat the
identity/past-volatility baselines}. On the FinCall temporal split the text ridge
head is near zero and collapses at $\tau{=}30$; the MLP heads overfit badly. On the
\tickerdisjoint{} split the text ridge head is weak ($\Rtwo$ between $-0.04$ and
$+0.17$) while the past-vol-only head is consistently strong
($\Rtwo\approx0.19$--$0.45$), so any apparent text gain is not separable from what
past volatility already provides. MAEC shows the same pattern at smaller magnitude
(Appendix~\ref{app:complete}).

\subsection{Identity controls (Stage 2 diagnostic)}
Table~\ref{tab:controls} explains the negative result. A linear probe recovers the
ticker from frozen call embeddings at 89.5\% on FinCall (319$\times$ chance) and
53.9\% on MAEC (487$\times$ chance): identity is heavily encoded. The transcript
shuffle is decisive---replacing each test call's transcript with a \emph{different
call from the same ticker} barely changes \Rtwo, whereas a global shuffle degrades
it. The heads are reading \emph{which company} is speaking, not \emph{what} is said.
This is the in-corpus confirmation of \citet{scss2024} and the reason RQ4 is central.

\subsection{Audio content (Stage 3)}
Table~\ref{tab:audio} extends the same design to audio. Audio-\emph{only} heads
(eGeMAPS, WavLM-Large, emotion2vec+, ridge or MLP) sit at \Rtwo{} zero on every
split and horizon ($-0.06$ to $+0.03$): frozen audio content alone carries no
measurable \dv{} signal. Adding past-volatility covariates reproduces the Stage-2
pattern---the only DM-significant wins over Stage-1 are past-vol-augmented heads on
the \tickerdisjoint{} split. The audio-shuffle control (bottom block) is decisive:
replacing each test call's audio with a random \emph{other company's} call leaves
most of those gains intact ($\tau{=}3$: $0.337\!\to\!0.318$; $\tau{=}30$:
$0.212\!\to\!0.100$), so the audio features are inert and past volatility carries
the signal. Identity is nonetheless encoded: a linear probe recovers the ticker from
per-call WavLM embeddings at 76.3\% (271$\times$ chance; emotion2vec+ 29.4\%,
105$\times$). An exploratory gender-confound check (eGeMAPS-F0 dominant-speaker
proxy, 100\% coverage) finds near-identical per-group test MSE (0.241 low-pitch
vs.\ 0.242 high-pitch) and a 0.03 correlation between speaker F0 and squared error:
with inert audio features, no large gender disparity is detectable (see the Ethics
Statement for caveats).

\subsection{Fusion and the consolidated grid (Stage 4)}
Gated fusion and late-fusion stacking over all frozen modalities answer RQ2 in the
negative: \emph{multimodal is not better than the best unimodal model}. Only one of
48 \dv{} test cells beats Stages 1--3 DM-significantly (gated fusion, temporal
$\tau{=}7$, $\Rtwo{=}+0.019$---negligible), and the best fusion cell overall
(stacking, \tickerdisjoint{} $\tau{=}3$, $\Rtwo{=}0.436$) is not significant
vs.\ Stage-1 ($p{=}0.13$). Table~\ref{tab:grid} consolidates the ladder with one
pre-registered canonical model per stage and Holm-corrected confirmatory tests. Two
facts summarise it. On the regime-honest \emph{temporal} split, no content stage
beats the econometric floor at any horizon; where a content model is
Holm-significantly better than Stage-1 (fusion at $\tau{=}30$) it merely beats a
collapsed GBDT while remaining below persistence. On the \emph{\tickerdisjoint}
split the favorable cells are exactly the past-vol-augmented ones that the shuffle
controls (Tables~\ref{tab:controls} and~\ref{tab:audio}) show to be
identity/past-vol-confounded. The per-year breakdown sharpens the regime point: the
FinCall temporal test year is entirely 2021, and there every content model
underperforms persistence on level-$v$ (HAR $-0.29$, GBDT $-0.82$, text $-1.09$,
audio $-1.66$, fusion $-1.02$).

\subsection{LLM structured features (Stage 5): the extraction fails its audit}
\label{sec:results-llm}
Stage 5 was pre-registered with a validity gate: corpus-scale extraction of the
structured features is blocked until the model's labels reach $\kappa>0.6$ against
human rubric labels on a frozen 50-call train-only audit sample, extracted through
the \emph{same} engine and configuration as the corpus run would use. The gate
failed decisively (Table~\ref{tab:llmkappa}): the three confirmatory fields score
$\kappa$ of 0.165 (guidance direction), $-0.050$ (hedging intensity---worse than
chance), and 0.222 (surprise mentions), with call-level-bootstrap 95\% upper bounds
all far below 0.6. Every output was 100\% schema-valid, so this is precisely the
gap between \emph{form} and \emph{content} validity: constrained decoding
guarantees parseable JSON, not meaningful measurements.

\input{tables/llm}

Four controls make the diagnosis actionable, and all four capability levers are
null. Sharpened prompt anchors move $\kappa$ by at most $+0.04$; near-lossless
Q8\_0 quantization (vs.\ 4-bit) and a 2$\times$ larger model (Qwen2.5-14B) both
produce differences whose bootstrap CIs all span zero (the 14B also fails the gate
on its own terms); the single measurable effect in the whole diagnostic set is
few-shot exemplars of the rater's own labels, which lift hedging from below chance
to $\kappa\approx0.09$ ($+0.092$ $[+0.029, +0.181]$)---a fifth of the distance to
the bar, on exactly the field whose written anchor omits a baseline. The failure
modes are structural rather than noisy: the model rates 47 of 50 Q\&A sections
exactly ``2'' on analyst tone (near-constant output), its hedging ratings are
statistically independent of the human's, and it misses 26 of 36 human-positive
surprise rows. Per the pre-registered rule, corpus-scale extraction was therefore
\emph{not} run: there is no Result Table 5, and RQ3 is answered at the validity
stage---the ``auditable semantics'' this sub-literature consumes did not survive
their audit. Notably, the closest prior system \citep{cao2024ecc} reports a 27.7\%
MSE reduction from LLM-extracted semantics with no human validation of extraction
quality at all, and the highest published agreement figure in this space
\citep{evasionbench2026} is inter-LLM, not human, agreement. Two caveats travel
with this finding: the gate scored a single careful rater (inter-annotator
agreement pending), and the 0.6 bar's implicit human--human ceiling was never
measured---subjective ordinal annotation routinely sits at human--human
$\kappa$ of 0.4--0.6, so the honest reporting unit going forward is model
$\kappa$ \emph{relative to} human--human $\kappa$ (see Limitations).
