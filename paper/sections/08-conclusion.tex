\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We revisit multimodal earnings-call volatility prediction with modern open-weight
models, strong econometric baselines, leakage-proof splits, and an explicit
identity-control suite, released as the \ecvol{} benchmark. Across the completed
ladder the evidence is consistent and negative: frozen text content reads company
identity rather than call content (89.5\% ticker-probe accuracy; within-ticker
shuffles leave performance unchanged); frozen audio content is inert beyond past
volatility (its apparent gains survive a global audio shuffle); fusion never
significantly beats the best unimodal model; and LLM-extracted ``auditable''
semantic features fail their pre-registered human-validity audit
($\kappa\le0.222$ vs.\ a 0.6 gate), so were never scaled to the corpus. The
pre-registered framing gate is provisionally resolved to rigorous re-examination:
what the leaderboard literature measures as content signal is, under honest
controls, overwhelmingly company identity plus past volatility---and the semantic
features it increasingly consumes are not validated measurements. The post-cutoff
lookahead evaluation (in progress) completes the diagnostic suite.
