\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We audited the evaluation substrate that a decade of multimodal earnings-call
volatility prediction shares---inherited label files with a zero-day embargo
against 30-day targets, majority company overlap between training and test, no
reported \tickerdisjoint{} condition, degenerate label cells, and a horizon
convention that downstream work misattributes---and released \ecvol-bench to
replace it: reconstructed identity and dates for the one corpus with open audio,
targets under both horizon conventions, embargoed and \tickerdisjoint{} splits,
strong econometric baselines, and an identity-control suite, all regenerable from
one command line. Run over modern open-weight text and audio representations, the
benchmark's answer is consistent and negative: frozen text content reads company
identity rather than call content (89.5\% ticker-probe accuracy; within-ticker
shuffles leave performance unchanged); frozen audio content is inert beyond past
volatility (its apparent gains survive a global audio shuffle); and fusion never
significantly beats the best unimodal model. What the leaderboard literature
measures as content signal is, under honest controls, overwhelmingly company
identity plus past volatility.
