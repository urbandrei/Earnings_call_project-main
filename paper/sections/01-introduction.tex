\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An earnings call is the one regular occasion on which a company's managers speak
at length about their business. Since \citet{qin2019what}, a line of papers has
used both the transcript and the audio of these calls to predict how volatile the
stock will be over the following 3 to 30 trading days, and HTML
\citep{yang2020html}, MAEC \citep{li2020maec}, VolTAGE \citep{sawhney2020voltage},
DialogueGAT \citep{sang2022dialoguegat}, KeFVP \citep{niu2023kefvp} and ECC
Analyzer \citep{cao2024ecc} all report beating classical forecasts.
\citet{scss2024} gave a reason to look again. On a corpus of their own, transcript
representations mostly told the model \emph{which company} was speaking, and on
that corpus and on the shared benchmarks a forecast from the company's own past
volatility matched or beat every transcript model.

What that leaves open is whether any published model keeps its score once the
company is taken away, and the files the field scores on cannot answer it. Several studies evaluated on EC and MAEC use redistributed label and split files. We audited them (\S\ref{sec:substrate}). They leave no gap between
training and test dates even though each target runs up to 30 days past its call.
Most test calls come from companies that are also in training, and no paper
reports what happens when they are not. They define horizons in calendar days,
which later papers describe as trading days. They put day~0 on the date of the
call, although most companies publish before the open, a defect noted by
\citet{scss2024} which no shared label set corrects. Their labels cannot be
regenerated from prices under any single stated rule. FinCall-Surprise
\citep{fincall2025}, which ships raw audio, has no ticker, date or speaker
fields, so the identity checks cannot be run on it at all. No published model had
been rerun under such checks, and no frozen volatility pipeline had been scored
on calls after its training cutoff (Figure~\ref{fig:problems}). This paper makes the diagnosis measurable on the
files in use and releases the tools for it.\footnote{Anonymized repository (decision
log, harness, checksums, ledger): \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/ANON-URL-TO-BE-FILLED}}

\paragraph{What we contribute.}
(1)~\textbf{A protocol over existing corpora}, released as \ecvol-bench. We
recover company identities and dates for FinCall-Surprise (92.9\% of calls, and a
60-call manual audit passes 60/60), time each release from 8-K filings, and ship
targets under both horizon definitions and a forward-looking variant, splits with
a 30-session gap, company-disjoint splits and one that applies both rules, and a
harness in which every table comes from one command (\S\ref{sec:benchmark}). It
redistributes no raw audio or transcripts.
(2)~\textbf{An audit and a toolkit.} We audit the shared label and split files
(Table~\ref{tab:substrate}) and the code of seventeen papers, rerun the five
runnable models under the same checks, on their own data where it is still
available, and release the checks themselves, company-level diagnostics for any
prediction file and probes and content swaps for any model one can run.
(3)~\textbf{Results} for modern open-weight text and audio models against a
matched price-only model, with a margin fixed in advance, and for the frozen
pipeline on 475 calls after every model's training cutoff
(\S\ref{sec:results}). Stages, checks and tests were recorded in a dated decision
log before the results existed.
