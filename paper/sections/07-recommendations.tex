\section{Recommendations and Outlook}
\label{sec:recommendations}

The results above say what an evaluation has to control for before a content
effect can be claimed, not that earnings calls carry no information. We suggest
that future work on this task report six things. A \textbf{company-disjoint}
split next to the temporal one. A \textbf{gap} of at least the longest horizon
between training and test. \textbf{Past-volatility baselines} as the reference
for every claim. The \textbf{same-company and random swaps} when the model is available,
otherwise the prediction-only diagnostics of the released audit command,
reported as descriptive. \textbf{Day~0 measured} from the release time. And the
\textbf{horizon definition} with every number. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:checklist}
gives the reason for each and where the release provides it. \ecvol-bench covers
all six for FinCall-Surprise and MAEC, and a quickstart script produces the
baseline, a prediction file and the audit report from a released archive.

Content signal may still be found once it is looked for in the right place.
Measured release times make \textbf{event-window targets} possible, where
language about guidance and surprise should matter most and same-company history
explains least. \textbf{Structured features} such as the transcript dimensions of
\citet{fastnumbers2026} can be audited one field at a time and tested for what
they add beyond the numbers. \textbf{Audio} deserves a
test on source-quality recordings with resolved speakers, and \textbf{additional
corpora} such as MM-FinEval \citep{mmfineval2026} are open to the identity
recovery used here.
