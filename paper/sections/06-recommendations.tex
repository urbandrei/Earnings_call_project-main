\section{Recommendations and Outlook}
\label{sec:recommendations}

The results above are not an argument against using earnings calls. They are an
argument about what an evaluation has to control for before a content effect can
be claimed. We suggest that future work on this task report six things. A
\textbf{company-disjoint} split next to the temporal one, since identity carries
most of the apparent signal. A \textbf{gap} between training and test dates of at
least the longest horizon. \textbf{Past-volatility baselines} (persistence and
HAR-RV, plus a constant on change targets) as the reference for every claim. The
\textbf{same-company and random swaps}, or the released audit command, so that a
gain can be attributed to the call rather than to the company. \textbf{Day~0
measured} from the release time rather than the call date. And the
\textbf{horizon definition}, calendar or trading days, stated with every number.
\ecvol-bench provides each of these for FinCall-Surprise and MAEC, and the audit
command applies the checks to any prediction file. The release also ships the
processed inputs as one archive per corpus (targets under both definitions and
anchors, the splits, the pooled text and audio features, the identity and timing
tables, with checksums) and a quickstart script that goes from an archive to the
past-volatility baseline, a prediction file and the audit report in a few
seconds, so a new model can be compared against the same floor and checks on the
first day.

There is also reason to expect that content signal can be found once it is
looked for in the right place. Measured release times make
\textbf{event-window targets} possible, such as the volatility of the first
sessions after the release, where language about guidance and surprise should
matter most and where same-company history explains least. \textbf{Structured
features} extracted from the call, such as guidance changes and the gap between
the quantitative surprise and management's framing of it, can be audited one
field at a time, unlike pooled embeddings, and can be combined with the fast
numeric signal that \citet{fastnumbers2026} show arrives first. \textbf{Audio}
has so far been tested only on lossy webcast transcodes without speaker
diarisation, so recordings at source quality with resolved speakers are the fair
test of prosody. \textbf{Larger company-disjoint corpora} are now within reach,
since MM-FinEval \citep{mmfineval2026} ships ticker and date fields and the checks
here transfer to it directly. Each of these can be evaluated against the
released baselines on the first day, which is what the benchmark is for.
