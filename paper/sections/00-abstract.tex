For a decade, papers have predicted how much a stock will move after
its earnings call from what executives say and how they sound, and
most report beating forecasts built from past volatility. Nearly all
are scored on one set of label and split files handed from paper to
paper. We audit those files and find they cannot support the claim: no
gap separates training from test although targets look 30 days ahead,
44--80\% of test calls come from companies seen in training, and day
zero sits on the call date although most companies release results
before the open. We release an evaluation protocol and an audit
toolkit that make these problems measurable. The protocol recovers
company identities and dates for a public audio corpus that ships
without them, times each release from SEC filings, and provides
company-disjoint and gapped splits; the toolkit runs identity checks
on any file of predictions. Under these checks, open text and audio
models add nothing to a past-volatility forecast: text embeddings
mostly identify the company (89.5\% probe accuracy), audio does no
better than a constant, and five published models rerun on their own
data see test error rise by 12--82\% once test companies are unseen,
while price-only models are unchanged. Scoring the frozen pipeline on
475 calls after every model's training cutoff changes none of this.
Content signal may still exist; it has to be shown to survive these
checks, and the released protocol, baselines and audit command make
that the starting point.
