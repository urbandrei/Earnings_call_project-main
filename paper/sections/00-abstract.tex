For about a decade, researchers have tried to predict how much a company's stock
will move in the weeks after its quarterly earnings call, using what executives
say on the call and how they sound. Many papers report that such models beat
classical forecasts built from past price swings. We find that the shared test
files behind these claims cannot support them. The files leave no gap between the
training and test periods even though every target looks 30 days ahead, 44--80\%
of their test calls come from companies the model also saw in training, and they
place ``day zero'' on the day of the call although most companies release their
numbers before the market opens that morning. We built \ecvol-bench to fix this.
It adds company names and dates to a public audio corpus that ships without
them, times each earnings release from SEC filings, provides targets under both
horizon definitions in use, gives splits that keep training and test apart in
time and in company, and includes a set of identity checks that run on any file
of predictions. With these checks in place, modern open text and audio models add
nothing to a forecast built from past volatility alone. Text embeddings mostly
identify which company is talking (89.5\% probe accuracy), audio features do no
better than a constant, and combining the two helps in one horizon out of eight.
When we rerun the field's own models on their own data, the text models lose
12--82\% in accuracy once test companies are unseen, while price-only models do
not move. Scoring the frozen pipeline on 475 calls from late 2025, after every
model's training cutoff, changes none of this. All code, metadata, splits,
targets, features and result tables are released.
