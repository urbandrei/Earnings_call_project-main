For about a decade, researchers have predicted how much a company's stock will
move after its quarterly earnings call from what executives say and how they
sound, and many report beating classical forecasts built from past price swings.
Several studies reuse EC/MAEC label and split files redistributed by earlier work. We audit those files and find that they cannot support
the claim. They leave no gap between the training and test periods although
targets extend up to 30 days ahead, most of their test calls come from companies
the model also saw in training, and they place day zero on the day of the call
although most companies release their numbers before the market opens. We release
an evaluation protocol and an audit toolkit that make these assumptions explicit
and testable. The protocol recovers company names and dates for a public audio
corpus that ships without them, times each release from SEC filings, provides
targets under both horizon definitions in use and a forward-looking variant, and
splits that keep training and test apart in time and in company. The toolkit
reports company-level diagnostics on any file of predictions. Under this protocol,
the open text and audio models we evaluate do not improve on a matched model that
uses past volatility alone by a margin fixed in advance, and their embeddings
mostly identify which company is speaking. Five published models rerun under the
same checks show a similar pattern, and a frozen pipeline scored on calls after
every model's training cutoff keeps the same ordering. Isolated gains remain, and
the released protocol, baselines and audit command make them the starting point
for the next study.
