For about a decade, researchers have predicted how much a company's stock will
move after its quarterly earnings call from what executives say and how they
sound, and many report beating classical forecasts built from past price swings.
Nearly all of those results are scored on one set of label and split files handed
down from paper to paper. We audit those files and find that they cannot support
the claim. They leave no gap between the training and test periods although every
target looks 30 days ahead. Between 44\% and 80\% of their test calls come from
companies the model also saw in training. And they place ``day zero'' on the day
of the call, although most companies release their numbers before the market
opens that morning. We release an evaluation protocol and an audit toolkit that
make these problems measurable. The protocol recovers company names and dates for
a public audio corpus that ships without them, times each release from SEC
filings, provides targets under both horizon definitions in use, and splits that
keep training and test apart in time and in company. The toolkit runs identity
checks on any file of predictions. Under these checks, modern open text and audio
models do not improve on a matched model that uses past volatility alone. Text
embeddings mostly identify which company is talking (89.5\% probe accuracy), and
on a date-ordered split with unseen companies no text, audio or combined model
reduces error against that matched model by a margin fixed in advance. Five
published models rerun under the same checks show a similar pattern. The test
error of their text models rises by 12--82\% once test companies are unseen,
while price-only models show no consistent change. Scoring the frozen pipeline on 475 calls from
late 2025, after every model's training cutoff, changes none of this. Content
signal may still exist in these calls. It has to be shown to survive these
checks, and the released protocol, baselines and audit command make that the
starting point for the next study.
