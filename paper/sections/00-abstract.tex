A decade of work predicts post-earnings-call stock volatility from the text and
audio of quarterly conference calls. We argue that the evaluation substrate this
literature shares cannot support its claims: the canonical benchmarks are scored
with a zero-day embargo against 30-day targets, with 44--80\% of test calls from
companies also present in training, on label files inherited unchanged from paper
to paper, and under a horizon convention that downstream work misattributes
\pending{regenerated by \texttt{ecvol audit substrate}, Table~\ref{tab:substrate}}.
We release \ecvol-bench, an open multimodal benchmark over FinCall-Surprise and
MAEC that repairs this substrate: reconstructed company identity and call dates for
a corpus that ships without them (92.9\% of calls; 95.3\% of earnings calls; 60/60
manual audit), three target families (level log-realised-volatility, change \dv,
HAR-residual) under \emph{both} the calendar-day and trading-day horizon
conventions, temporal and \tickerdisjoint{} splits with a 30-trading-day embargo,
SHA-256 manifests, strong econometric baselines, and an identity-control suite
(linear ticker probes, same-ticker and global content shuffles). Run over modern
open-weight text and audio representations, the benchmark returns a consistent
answer: text embeddings encode \emph{which company} is speaking (89.5\%
ticker-probe accuracy; within-ticker transcript shuffles leave performance
unchanged), frozen audio content is inert beyond past volatility (its apparent
gains survive even a global audio shuffle), and fusion never significantly beats
the best unimodal model---under honest controls, apparent content signal in this
task family is overwhelmingly company identity plus past volatility.
\pending{We then re-run the field's five publicly runnable models on their own
data under these controls (Table~\ref{tab:repro}), re-evaluate the audio ladder
on clean 2025 recordings, and evaluate the frozen pipeline on
post-LLM-cutoff calls.}
All code, configs, manifests, reconstructed metadata, split files, targets,
derived features, and result tables are released.
