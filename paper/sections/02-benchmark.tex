\section{The Shared Test Files and the Benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\paragraph{What the field evaluates on.}
\label{sec:substrate}
The EarningsCall (EC) benchmark of \citet{qin2019what} and the two MAEC cohorts
are scored on split and label files that VolTAGE and KeFVP redistribute. We
audited those files (Table~\ref{tab:substrate}). The VolTAGE and KeFVP test files
are byte for byte the same file, so the whole line of work shares one label set
that was never recomputed from prices. Five of the six boundaries between
training, validation and test have a zero-day gap (the sixth has three days)
while the targets reach 30 days past each call. On the time-ordered splits the field reports, 80.4\% (EC), 44.2\% (MAEC-15)
and 55.7\% (MAEC-16) of test calls are from companies that also appear in
training. Any time-ordered split allows that. The problem is that no paper reports
the company-disjoint case, so the confound of \citet{scss2024} has never been
measured on these benchmarks. The original paper defines its horizons in calendar
days, but most later work describes them as trading days (21 sessions rather than
30 at $\tau{=}30$). Finally, the shipped MAEC labels correlate only 0.52--0.87 with
calendar-day targets we recompute from prices under the same call-date anchor
(0.25--0.66 at $\tau{=}3$). We could not reproduce them under any single stated
rule.

\paragraph{Corpora and company identity.}
\textbf{FinCall-Surprise} \citep{fincall2025} is the primary corpus, with 2{,}688
calls from 2019--2021 and MP3 audio, transcripts and slides. It ships four fields
per record and no ticker, date, CIK or speaker names. We recover the company and
the date from the transcript text and the slide metadata, check them against price
data, and release the table with its 80 manual corrections. 2{,}496 calls resolve
(92.9\% overall, 95.3\% of the earnings-type calls), and a 60-call manual audit
passes 60/60. \textbf{MAEC} \citep{li2020maec} is the secondary corpus, with about
3{,}443 calls from 2015--2018, sentence-aligned audio features and no raw audio.
\textbf{Earnings25} \citep{jiang2026earnings25} has 514 S\&P~500 calls from
July--December 2025, with webcast MP3 at 64\,kbps for 281 calls and at most
24\,kbps for 232. It postdates every model's training data, and 475 of its calls
map to a ticker and join to prices.

\paragraph{Targets.}
Daily adjusted prices come from a free source and were checked against Tiingo on
50 tickers. Let $r_t$ be the return on session $t$, counted from day~0, the last
session before the call's information is public. The main target is
$v_{\mathrm{post}}(\tau)=\ln\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}(r_t-\bar r)^2}$ for
$\tau\in\{3,7,15,30\}$, and $v_{\mathrm{pre}}(\tau)$ is the same quantity over the
sessions ending at day~0. Under the \textbf{trading-day} definition $n=\tau$. Under
the \textbf{calendar-day} definition of \citet{qin2019what} the window holds every
session within $\tau$ calendar days, so $n\approx0.7\tau$, and at $\tau{=}3$ a
Thursday or Friday call has at most one session. We drop any window with fewer
than two sessions and record the reason. That removes 42.5\% of FinCall's calls at
$\tau{=}3$ calendar days, for which the released label sets still carry a value.
The two definitions are not interchangeable. $v_{\mathrm{post}}$ correlates only
0.78 between them at $\tau{=}3$, and on the temporal splits the persistence MSE is
44--63\% higher there under calendar days. A published MSE therefore means little
unless its definition is stated (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:convention}). Every result below uses trading
days, and the definition is a column in every result table. We add two targets
that are harder to game with company identity. The \textbf{change}
$\Delta v=v_{\mathrm{post}}-v_{\mathrm{pre}}$ subtracts the company's own level,
and the \textbf{HAR-residual} subtracts a HAR-RV forecast. After joining to
prices, FinCall has 2{,}433 calls with a target (97.5\%) and MAEC 2{,}688 (78.6\%,
the rest lacking usable price history).

\paragraph{When the information became public.}
\label{sec:timing}
Almost no transcript states a clock time, so we take the moment the information
became public from EDGAR. It is the acceptance time of the earliest 8-K filing
with Item~2.02 (the earnings release) within three days before to one day after
the call. That covers 89.3\% of FinCall, 68.6\% of MAEC and 99.8\% of Earnings25. The
rest fall back, in recorded order, to Earnings25's call-start time, the
before/after-market flag of \citet{scss2024}, and an after-hours assumption. A
release before the open makes the call date itself day~1. Compared with the
call-date anchor that every released label set uses, \textbf{day~0 moves for 53\%
of FinCall, 56\% of MAEC and 72\% of Earnings25 calls}, almost always one session
earlier. Under the published anchor, the session in which the market reacts
therefore sits in the \emph{pre} window for most calls. On the measured anchor the
persistence MSE is up to 11\% higher on FinCall, 25\% on MAEC and 27\% on
Earnings25, and $\Delta v$ gains a positive mean (on FinCall $+0.52$ at $\tau{=}3$
and $+0.10$ at $\tau{=}30$). The call-date targets ship as a labelled variant.

\paragraph{Splits and release.}
\emph{Temporal} splits (about 70/10/20 in date order) leave a 30-trading-day gap
between segments so that no target window crosses a boundary.
\emph{\tickerdisjoint} splits keep every validation and test company out of
training. Their overlap is 0.0\%, against 91.3\% (FinCall) and 79.4\% (MAEC) on
our own temporal splits. The harness fails any feature that reads price data after
the call's as-of time, and leakage assertions run in CI. The release contains all
of the above, the derived-feature archives, the harness, and every result CSV with
its run record (resolved config, git commit, seeds and file hash). Raw audio and
transcripts are never redistributed.
